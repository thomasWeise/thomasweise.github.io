Recently, the fourth *Space Optimization Competition&nbsp;([SpOC](https://www.esa.int/gsp/ACT/news/spoc-2026))* organized by the [Advanced Concepts Team](https://www.esa.int/gsp/ACT) of the European Space Agency&nbsp;([European Space Agency](https://www.esa.int)) ended.
This [competition](https://github.com/esa/SpOC4) was about applying optimization algorithms to problems from space {% include taglink.liquid tag="logistics" %} and {% include taglink.liquid tag="or" tag_text="Operations Research" %}.
It had three challenges,

1. a [multi-route logistics problem](https://optimise.esa.int/challenge/spoc-4-luna-tomato-logistics/About), where the goal was to discover a set of routes forming a logistics network from Earth orbits to Moon orbits to the Moon surface,
2. a [single-route logistics problem](https://optimise.esa.int/challenge/spoc-4-keplerian-tomato-traveling-salesperson/About), where the goal was to find an efficient tour that visits `n`&nbsp;satellites in the moon orbit, and
3. a [tie-breaker challenge](https://optimise.esa.int/challenge/spoc-4-luna-tomato-advertising/About), where the goal was to configure a set of spacecraft on certain Moon orbits to form a Morse code message by covering the moonlight.

Each of these challenges involved calculations regarding time, velocity changes, gravity, and orbits using integration of differential equations, trajectory computations, and that alike.
Challenge&nbsp;1 is divided into two types of problems, one&nbsp;("beginner") being strictly {% include taglink.liquid tag="combinatorial_optimization" tag_text="combinatorial" %} and the other one additionally involving rather complex physics.

Despite having no background at all in any of these topics, we gave this challenge a go as team [ScholORs_HFUU+Sunway](https://optimise.esa.int/user/dd46ba62e88c45b7958a97d529e3a220).
We did this mainly for fun and worked together remotely at three different locations, namely at [Hefei University](https://www.hfuu.edu.cn/english)&nbsp;{%- include lang.liquid text="合肥大学" url="https://www.hfuu.edu.cn" %}, at Sunway University&nbsp;(Malaysia, {% include person.liquid person="abdipoor_sina" %}), and BUPT&nbsp;International School, Queen Mary University&nbsp;({% include person.liquid person="li_mingxuan" %}).

To be honest, the physics behind the challenges were intractable for us.
Since each of us is concurrently working on entirely different topics in our research and study areas, we also had limited time to invest into these challenges.
Yet, we found them very interesting and wanted to know how far we can get with our ability to work the "{% include taglink.liquid tag="optimization" %} aspects" of the challenges but not the "physics aspects".
Space travel, after all, is one of the coolest things (not) on earth.

In the end, we arrived at the 9th&nbsp;place, which is not very good.
Then again, we found feasible and not-too-bad solutions for every single challenge and problem instance.
For most of the tasks, the vast majority of solutions are infeasible.
Plugging the objective function into an optimization algorithm with the hope that the algorithm will sort it out and discover feasible solutions eventually is not going to work.

Surprisingly, for several of the challenges, we were the first team that found any solution.
We even managed to hold rank&nbsp;1 on several challenges and probably overall for several weeks, too.
For participating just for fun, I think that's not bad.

For most of the tasks, we used simple {% include taglink.liquid tag="local_search" tag_text="local searches" %} like {% include taglink.liquid tag="RLS" %} and {% include taglink.liquid tag="SA" tag_text="Simulated Annealing" %} with some special encodings and preprocessing.
The reason is that the objective function&nbsp;(FEs) evaluations here were very costly, often involving integration of differential equation or otherwise "simulating" trajectories.
Without better Maths and Physics understanding, there was no way around paying this cost &mdash; and this probably was one of the factors limiting our success.
Anyway, if only few FEs can be done, then it is best to spend them on strict local search.
Combining this approach with some preprocessing, we could find a feasible solution within only 187&nbsp;seconds for the [largest instance](https://optimise.esa.int/challenge/spoc-4-keplerian-tomato-traveling-salesperson/p/large) of the second challenge on an off-the-shelf laptop computer, for example.
